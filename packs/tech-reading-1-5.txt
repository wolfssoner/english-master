#סיפור The Cyberattack on the City Office | קשה
One morning, the employees of a city office came to work and found a strange message on every screen: "Your files are inaccessible. Pay 5,000 in the next three days, or your files will be deleted." The computers worked, but nobody could open a single file. Every file had a new and unfamiliar format.

The office called a cybersecurity technician, Dana. She explained that this type of cyberattack became frequent in the last few years. The malware does not steal the files; it changes them so that only the criminals can open them again. After that, the criminals ask for money.

Dana did not recommend paying. "When an office pays, there is no guarantee that the criminals will give the files back," she said. "And every time an office pays, the criminals learn that this trick works." Instead, she asked a simple question: "Where are the copies of your files?"

The answer was not simple. The office kept copies on an external disk, but the disk stayed on the same computers all the time. As a result, the malware got to the external disk too, and its files were also inaccessible. However, one employee, Omer, had a habit of taking a copy of the most important directories home every Friday on a separate disk. His disk was at his home during the cyberattack, so it was safe.

With Omer's disk, the office recovered most of its files. Only the work of three days was lost. After the cyberattack, the office changed its strategy. Now there are three copies of every important file, and at least one of them is always separate from the office computers.

#סיפור The Temporary Files That Took the Disk | קשה
Ron is a technician in a small office. One afternoon, an employee called him. "My computer says that the disk has no free space, but I deleted my old pictures last week," she said. Ron was surprised. The computer was new, and its disk was big.

Ron looked at the disk and discovered the cause. A program that the employee used every day created temporary files. Temporary files are files that a program uses for a short time, such as while you edit a text. Normally, the program deletes these files when it closes. But this program had a fault. When it closed, it did not delete its temporary files. Every day, it left more files in a directory that was not visible, and after a year, there were more than 200,000 temporary files on the disk.

The employee did not see these files because the directory was not visible in the usual view. She only saw that the space on the disk became smaller and smaller, and she thought that her pictures were the problem.

Ron deleted the temporary files, and the disk had a lot of free space again. However, he knew that this was only a temporary solution. In a year, the problem would come back. So he informed the developers of the program about the fault, and he created a small automatic process that deletes old temporary files every week.

Three months later, the developers sent an update that fixed the fault. Ron kept his weekly process too. "A second layer of protection is never unnecessary," he said.

#סיפור The Faulty Disk That Gave a Warning | קשה
A scientist, Yael, kept the data of four years of her studies on one disk in her lab. For a few weeks, the disk made a strange noise, and it was often slow. Yael did not think that the problem was serious. The computer still worked, and all her files opened.

One day, a technician came to the lab to fix a different computer. He heard the noise and asked Yael about it. "That noise is a warning," he said. "Disks have moving parts, and when a part begins to break, the disk often becomes slow and makes noise before it stops. This disk will probably stop soon."

Yael was very anxious. The work of four years was on that disk, and she had no copy. The technician recommended a quick action: stop using the disk for new work and copy the most important data first. They started the copy that same afternoon. The process was very slow, because the disk had trouble reading some files. After two days, they had copied almost all the data. Only a few files had data corruption, and Yael could create them again from her notes.

A week later, the disk stopped. Nobody could retrieve a single file from it.

Yael learned two lessons. First, a device can work and still be at risk. Second, the time to make a copy is before the problem, not after it. Now she keeps her work in three places, and every month she checks that the three copies are identical.

#סיפור A Smart Device That Listens | קשה
Many families now have a smart device in the kitchen or in the living room. You say its name, and then you ask a question: "What is the weather today?" or "Tell me a short story." The device answers in a second. It seems like a trick, but the mechanism is simple, and it is important to understand it.

The device listens all the time, but only for one word: its name. While it waits for its name, it does not send what it hears. When it hears its name, it begins to send the next few seconds of sound to the computers of its provider. There, a program changes the sound into text, finds an answer, and sends the answer back to the device.

The problem is that the device is not perfect. Sometimes it hears a word that sounds like its name, and it sends a part of a private conversation to the provider. A family can be talking about money, about an illness, or about personal problems, and nobody in the room knows that a few seconds were sent. In addition, some providers keep these short sound files for a long time, and in the past, some employees of providers listened to parts of them to improve the product.

This does not tell us that smart devices are necessarily harmful. They are useful, in particular for people who cannot use a screen easily. But users should make informed decisions. They can check which sound files the provider keeps, delete their history, and turn off the device during private conversations. A smart device is a guest in the house, and a guest should not hear everything.

#סיפור The Translation That Lost Its Meaning | קשה
A small store had a new device for hot water. Its instructions came in English. To pay less, the owner used a free translation program to create instructions for local customers. The program worked fast, and the new text looked correct. Every sentence looked like a normal sentence, and nobody in the store noticed a problem.

A month later, a customer called the store. She did not understand the instructions. They told her to "leave the device near water for safety." In the original text, the sentence said the opposite: "for safety, do not leave the device near water." The translation program had lost one small word, "not", and the meaning of the sentence completely changed.

The owner asked a professional translator to check the whole text. She found 11 more mistakes. Most were small, but two were serious. In one place, the program translated a technical word with a meaning from another field. In another place, it translated a sentence word by word, so the new sentence was correct as a sentence but did not say what the original said.

The translator explained how a program and a person are different. A translation program is very good at frequent, simple sentences. However, it does not always understand context: who reads the text, what the product does, and which mistake can be harmful. A person who knows the field can notice when a sentence is correct as a sentence but incorrect in its meaning.

The owner did not stop using the program. He still uses it for messages and short texts. But now, every text about safety goes to a person before it goes to a customer.

#סיפור The Man Who Walked Behind the Employee | קשה
The office of a big engineering team has a strong entrance. Every employee has an identification card, and the door opens only when an employee touches the door with a card. The system also keeps a history of every entrance, so the office knows who entered and when.

One morning, a man who looked like a technician walked to the door with a big box in his hands. An employee, Noa, opened the door with her card. The man smiled and said, "Thank you, my hands are full." Noa kept the door open for him, and he walked in behind her. Nobody asked for his identification.

The man was not a technician. He was a cybersecurity professional. The office had asked him to check how safe it really was. In less than an hour, he walked into three rooms, took pictures of private papers on tables, and left a small device near a computer. Then he walked out of the same door.

In his explanation, the professional said that the problem was not the system. The cards and the door worked perfectly. The weak part was a human habit. People like to be helpful, and it feels strange to close a door in front of a person with full hands. Criminals know this, and they use it.

Nobody was angry with Noa. Instead, the office changed its design and gave every employee a short lesson. Now there is a second door, and only one person can pass at a time. Employees also learned a new habit: being helpful is good, but every guest waits at the entrance, and an employee confirms who he is before he enters.

#סיפור Local Storage or a Provider? | קשה
Every person who uses a computer must decide where to keep files. There are two main ways. The first is local storage: the files stay on the disk of your own computer or on an external disk at home. The second is storage with a provider: the files are on the computers of a big provider, and you can retrieve them from any device after you sign in.

Local storage is good for three reasons. It is fast, because the files are near you. It works when the provider is unavailable. And it is private: your files are not on the computers of another person. However, local storage has a serious problem. If the disk breaks, or the computer is stolen, or there is water in the house, the files can be lost.

Storage with a provider solves many of these problems. Providers keep multiple copies of every file in other places, so the loss of one disk is not the loss of your data. You can also open your files from anywhere, and synchronization keeps them identical on all your devices. But this way has its own risks. You pay a subscription every month. You depend on the provider: if it is unavailable, you cannot retrieve your files. And your private files are on computers that you cannot see or touch.

So which way is the best? Many professionals say that the question itself is incorrect. The safest strategy is to use both. Keep your files locally because it is fast and private, and keep a copy with a provider for recovery. In this way, one problem cannot take all your data.

#סיפור Multiple Layers of Protection | בינוני

In less than a minute, the file was on 12 computers. Tamar noticed the mistake after an hour and deleted the file. But deletion in one place does not delete the copies that people opened or copied during that hour. Two employees had opened the file, and one had copied it to his phone.

The office had to explain the mistake to all the employees, and Tamar felt terrible. The technician was not angry with her. Instead, he changed the design: private files about employees are now in a separate system, and nobody can move them to a shared directory by accident.

#סיפור Temporary Access That Stayed | בינוני
A store needed help with its system, so the owner gave an external technician access to the computers of the store. The idea was that the access would be temporary: one week. The technician fixed the problem in three days, and everyone forgot about the access.

Two years later, criminals entered the system of the store. The investigators discovered that they did not enter the store directly. First, they entered the computer of the technician, and from there they used his old access to the store. Nobody had deleted it.

Professionals call this a forgotten door. Every person who receives access to a system is a possible door for criminals. For this reason, temporary access should have a day of end in the system itself, and it should end automatically. The owner of the store now checks every three months who has access, and he deletes every access that is no longer necessary.

#סיפור The Data That Nobody Deleted | בינוני
A small store with a website kept the information of every customer who bought a product: names, locations, and phones. The owner thought that more data is always useful. One day, he thought, he would send them a message about a new product.

After eight years, the store had the information of 90,000 customers. Most of them had bought only once, many years before. Then criminals entered the system and stole everything. Now the owner had to inform 90,000 people that their personal information was stolen, although most of them had not bought there for years.

A cybersecurity professional explained the lesson. "Data that you do not keep cannot be stolen," she said. Many offices and stores now make a clear decision about retention: they keep personal data only for the time that it is necessary, and then they delete it.

#סיפור Identical Files Everywhere | בינוני
An office with 20 employees noticed that its storage was almost full, although the employees did not create many new files. A technician checked the storage and discovered a surprising result: almost half of the files were identical copies.

The reason was a habit. When an employee wanted to share a file, he sent it as a message to five or six people. Each person stored the file in his own directory. Then, when someone edited the file, he sent the new file again, and again everyone stored it. One file existed in 70 copies.

This habit did not only take space. It also created confusion. Nobody knew which copy was the latest one, and two times the office sent a customer an old file with incorrect prices. The technician recommended a simple change: keep one file in a shared directory, and send people the location of the file instead of the file itself.
