#סיפור Why Software Projects Are Almost Always Late | קשה
Ask any experienced developer how long a project will take, and you will probably receive an estimate that is too optimistic. Research on software projects shows that a significant majority of them finish later than planned, and many of them cost much more than the planned amount of money. Why is it so difficult to estimate software work?

The first reason is that software is not built from familiar parts. When a company builds a house, it can rely on decades of experience with similar houses. In software, almost every project contains something new: a new feature, a new technology, or old technologies used in a new way. The parts that are new are exactly the parts that are hardest to estimate.

The second reason is that the needs of the customer change. At the beginning of a project, customers often do not know exactly what they want. When they see the first version, they ask for changes. Each change seems small, but together they can double the size of the project.

The third reason is psychological. People tend to imagine the ideal case: the code works the first time, nobody is sick, and no unexpected problem appears. In reality, every project faces obstacles that nobody predicted. A developer who estimates "two weeks" is usually thinking about the work itself, not about meetings, bugs, and interruptions.

Experienced teams use several approaches to reduce this problem. They divide large projects into small parts that are easier to estimate. They compare new work to similar work from the past, instead of relying on intuition. And they add extra time for problems that they cannot predict. Most importantly, they treat an estimate as a range, not as a promise. "Between four and eight weeks" sounds less good than "six weeks," but it is much more honest.

#סיפור The Hidden Cost of Quick Code | קשה
Every developer knows the situation. A feature must be ready by Friday, and there are two ways to build it. The first way is clean and well designed, but it will take two weeks. The second way is a shortcut: it works, but the code is messy and hard to change. When there is pressure, many teams choose the shortcut and promise to fix it later.

Developers call the result "technical debt." The term compares bad code to money that you borrow. When you borrow money, you can buy something today, but you must pay interest later. In the same way, a shortcut lets a team release a feature quickly, but every future change in that part of the code becomes slower and more risky. The "interest" is the extra time that developers spend because the code is hard to understand.

Technical debt is not always bad. Sometimes a company needs to release a product quickly to test an idea, and it does not know yet whether the product will succeed. In that case, a shortcut can be a reasonable decision. The problem begins when the debt is never paid. Shortcut after shortcut, the code becomes so complex that nobody wants to touch it. New developers need months to understand it, and every small change creates new bugs.

Many successful teams manage technical debt in a planned way. They write down every shortcut and the reason for it. They keep a fixed part of their time, for example one day every two weeks, to improve old code. And they explain the cost to managers in business terms: not "the code is messy," but "this feature takes three days to change instead of one."

In the end, technical debt is a tool. Used carefully, it can help a team move fast. Ignored, it can slowly stop a team completely.

#סיפור Can We Trust the Opinions of Customers Online? | קשה
Before people buy a product, choose a hotel, or go to a restaurant, most of them read the opinions of other customers online. Research suggests that a product with a rating of 4.5 stars sells significantly more than a similar product with 4 stars. For this reason, these opinions have become extremely valuable, and where there is value, there is also fraud.

Fake opinions are a large industry. Some sellers pay people to write positive opinions about their own products. Others pay for negative opinions about their competitors. In some countries, there are companies that offer thousands of opinions for a fixed price. More recently, artificial intelligence has made the problem more serious, because it can produce hundreds of different, natural opinions in minutes.

Platforms try to fight this problem in several ways. They use algorithms to identify suspicious patterns: many opinions that appear on the same day, opinions from new accounts with no history, or opinions that use very similar language. Some platforms allow opinions only from customers who really bought the product. However, the people who create fake opinions also adapt, and the competition between the two sides continues.

What can a careful customer do? Experts suggest several strategies. Do not rely only on the final rating; read a variety of opinions, in particular the negative ones, which are harder to fake and often more specific. Pay attention to the details: a real opinion usually describes a specific experience, while a fake one is often general and very excited. And be suspicious when a product has hundreds of perfect opinions but almost no criticism.

No method is perfect. But a customer who reads carefully is much less likely to be deceived than one who looks only at the stars.

#סיפור Will Passwords Disappear? | קשה
For decades, the password has been the main way to prove who we are online. Yet passwords have serious weaknesses. People choose simple passwords, use the same password on many websites, and give them away in phishing attacks. According to several security reports, a large share of successful attacks begin with a stolen password.

A new technology, called a passkey, aims to replace passwords completely. Instead of a secret that you remember, a passkey uses a pair of keys. One key, the private key, remains on your device and never leaves it. The other key, the public key, is stored by the website. When you log in, the website sends a challenge, and your device uses the private key to answer it. The website can check the answer with the public key, but it never receives the private key itself. To unlock the private key, you simply use your fingerprint, your face, or the PIN of your device.

The advantages are significant. There is nothing to remember and nothing to type. Even if hackers steal the database of a website, they find only public keys, which are useless without the private keys. And because each passkey is connected to a specific website, a phishing website cannot receive it.

However, the change will not happen in one day. Many people do not understand the technology and are worried about what will happen if they lose their phone. Not all websites support passkeys yet. And some users are worried that passkeys make them more dependent on the large companies that make their devices.

Experts suggest that passwords and passkeys will exist side by side for many years. The direction, however, seems clear: the future of logging in is likely to be something that you have and something that you are, rather than something that you remember.

#סיפור The Algorithm That Knows What You Like | קשה
When you open a video platform or a social media application, the content that you see is not there by chance. It is selected by an algorithm that recommends content. The algorithm studies your behavior: which videos you watch until the end, which posts you like, how long you stop on each picture. Then it predicts which content will keep you on the platform for the longest time, and it shows you more of it.

From the point of view of the company, this approach is extremely effective. The longer users stay, the more advertisements they see, and the more money the company earns. For users, too, there are real benefits. The algorithm can help them discover music, ideas, and people that they would never find on their own.

However, researchers have identified several potential problems. The first is the "filter bubble." If the algorithm shows you only content that matches your previous interests and opinions, you may stop seeing other points of view. Over time, your picture of the world can become small, and you may believe that most people think like you.

The second problem is that the content that keeps people watching is not always good for them. Studies suggest that content that creates strong emotions, especially anger and fear, tends to receive more attention. An algorithm that is designed only to increase attention may therefore show more extreme content, even if nobody planned this result.

Some platforms now give users more choice: they can see why a post appeared, delete the history that the algorithm uses, or choose a simple order by time. Experts argue that this is a good beginning, but that the bigger question remains open. Should an algorithm aim only to keep us watching, or should it also consider what is good for us?

#סיפור The Energy Behind the Cloud | קשה
When we save a photo "in the cloud," it feels as if the photo goes to no real place. In reality, it goes to a data center: a huge building full of servers that work day and night. There are thousands of such centers around the world, and together they consume a significant amount of the electricity that the world produces. Some estimates suggest that data centers use between one and two percent of global electricity, and the number is growing.

Servers need electricity for two main purposes. The first is computing itself: every search, every video and every message requires processors to work. The second is cooling. Servers produce a lot of heat, and if they become too hot, they fail. In older data centers, the energy for cooling was sometimes almost equal to the energy for computing.

The rapid growth of artificial intelligence has increased the pressure. Training a large language model requires thousands of powerful processors that work for weeks. After training, every question that users ask the model also requires energy. As a result, several large technology companies have reported that their energy use increased significantly in recent years.

The industry is trying to respond in various ways. Companies build data centers in cold countries, where the outside air can cool the servers. They design new chips that do more work with less power. Some companies buy energy from wind and solar sources. In addition, engineers are developing smaller models that can provide similar results with much less computing.

This does not mean that we should stop using digital services. But it shows us that the cloud is not a cloud at all. It is a physical industry with physical costs, and every click has a small price that someone, in some place, pays.

#סיפור Designing for Everyone | קשה
Imagine that you cannot see the screen of your phone. How would you read a message, buy a ticket, or check your bank account? For millions of blind people, this is a real question. They use a screen reader: a program that reads the content of the screen aloud. A screen reader can work well, but only if the developers designed the application with it in mind.

This is the idea of accessibility: designing technology that people with different abilities can use. Accessibility includes people who cannot see or hear, people who cannot use a mouse, people with difficulties in reading, and older people who do not see as well as before. According to some estimates, about one in six people in the world has a significant disability.

Many accessibility problems are simple to fix. A button that is only an icon, with no text, has no meaning for a screen reader, which can only say "button." An image without a short description is invisible to a blind user. Text with light grey letters on a white background is hard to read for many people. A form that people can complete only with a mouse does not allow users who rely on the keyboard to use it.

Interestingly, designs that help people with a disability often help everyone. Subtitles in videos were created for deaf people, but today many people use them on the bus or in a room with a lot of noise. Clear language helps users who read slowly, and it also helps users who simply do not have much time.

In several countries, the law now requires accessibility for the websites of governments and large companies. But experts argue that the strongest reason is not the law. It is the simple principle that technology should not leave anyone behind.

#סיפור How Companies Decide With Two Versions | קשה
How does a large website decide whether a big button is better than a small one, or whether a shorter form will bring more customers? In the past, such decisions often came from the opinion of the most important person in the room. Today, many companies use a method called A/B testing.

The principle is simple. The company creates two versions of a page: version A, the current one, and version B, with one change. Then it divides its users by chance into two groups. One group sees version A, and the other sees version B. After some time, the company compares the results. If significantly more users of version B complete a purchase, the company has evidence that the change works.

The power of this method is that it measures what people actually do, not what they say. When companies ask them, users often say that they prefer one design, but their behavior shows the opposite. A/B testing also protects companies from their own intuition, which is often wrong. Some large companies report that most of the ideas that their experts believed in did not improve the results at all.

However, the method has limits. First, it requires many users. A small website with a few hundred users a day may need months to reach a reliable result. Second, it measures short-term behavior. A design that increases clicks today may make users angry and reduce their trust over time. Third, if a company runs too many tests and looks only at the positive results, some of those results will be only chance.

A/B testing is therefore a valuable tool, but it cannot replace thinking. Good teams use it to check their ideas, not to decide what their ideas should be.

#סיפור The Phone Call at Three in the Morning | בינוני
Large online services must work 24 hours a day. When something breaks at three in the morning, someone must respond. For this reason, many companies use an "on-call" system: every week, one or two engineers have the job of responding to serious alerts, even at night and on weekends.

Being on call can create a lot of pressure. An engineer may be woken up several times in one night, and he must make important decisions while he is tired. If alerts are frequent and many of them are false, engineers become exhausted, and they may begin to ignore warnings.

Experienced teams try to make on-call work possible for a long time. They divide the responsibility fairly between the people in the team. They remove alerts that do not require a real action. They write clear instructions for common problems, so that a tired engineer does not need to think from zero. And after every serious night, they ask a simple question: how can we make sure that this alert never wakes anyone again?

#סיפור The Backup That Nobody Tested | בינוני
A small company made a backup of its database every night for three years. The process was automatic, and every morning the system sent an email: "Backup completed successfully." Nobody was worried about data loss.

Then a hardware failure damaged the main database completely. The technician opened the latest backup, and discovered that it was empty. In fact, every backup from the last eight months was empty. After an update, the backup program had lost permission to read the database. It continued to run every night and to create files, but the files contained nothing. And because the program did not check the content, it continued to report success.

The company recovered part of its data from an old backup, but eight months of work were lost. The lesson is famous in the world of engineers: a backup that you have never restored is not a backup; it is only a wish. Experienced teams therefore test their backups often, by actually restoring them to a separate system and checking that the data is complete.

#סיפור Why Video Meetings Make Us Tired | בינוני
Since many people began to work from home, video meetings have become part of daily routine. Many workers report that a day of video meetings makes them more tired than a day of meetings in an office. Researchers have suggested several reasons for this.

First, in a video meeting we look at faces from a very short distance for a long time, something that rarely happens in real life. Second, we see our own face on the screen all the time, and many people check how they look again and again. Third, we lose many of the small signals that we use in a normal conversation, such as small movements of the hands, so the brain must work harder to understand others. Finally, there is no natural break: one meeting often begins one minute after the previous one ended.

Some simple changes can help. People can hide their own picture, turn off the camera when it is not necessary, and plan short breaks between meetings. Some companies have also decided that certain meetings can simply be an email.
